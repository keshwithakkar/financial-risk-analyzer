LOAN RISK POLICY

1. Credit Risk:
Applicants with a previous loan default may have a higher risk of future default.

2. Income:
Higher and stable income generally indicates better repayment capacity.

3. Loan Amount:
A high loan amount compared with the applicant's income may increase repayment risk.

4. Loan-to-Income Ratio:
A higher percentage of income used for loan repayment can indicate increased financial risk.

5. Employment:
Longer employment history can indicate greater income stability.

6. Credit History:
A longer credit history can provide more information about an applicant's repayment behavior.

7. Interest Rate:
Higher interest rates can increase the total repayment burden and may increase default risk.

8. Loan Grade:
Loan grades represent the estimated credit quality of a borrower. Lower grades generally indicate higher credit risk.

9. Previous Default:
A previous default recorded in the applicant's credit history is an important risk factor.

10. Risk Assessment:
Loan risk should be evaluated using multiple factors rather than relying on a single factor.

IMPORTANT:
This document provides general educational guidelines for demonstrating an AI-powered financial risk analysis system. It is not financial advice and should not be used as the sole basis for lending decisions.
